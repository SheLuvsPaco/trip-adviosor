# Route 10 — The Temples, Follies & Working Machines Loop

Research-complete tenth dataset entry for October 4-15, 2026. The group collects two cars at Boston Logan and departs on October 4, returns both cars and sleeps near Logan on October 14, and protects October 15 for the airport. The route is entirely within the United States with no Canada or J-1 re-entry dependency.

## Locked route

Boston Logan → Rockville/Farmington/East Milford → Robbinsville/Hamilton → Winterthur/Wilmington/Baltimore → Triangle/Stafford/North Richmond → Danville → Roanoke → Mount Crawford/Harrisonburg → Carlisle/Harrisburg/Hershey → Stroudsburg/Montville/Skylands → Danbury → Boston Logan.

- 11 calendar days and 11 hotel nights, including Boston on October 14
- 1,547 OSRM baseline road miles
- 24 core, separately rateable places; all 11 former hidden-gem replacements are now main stops
- 80 visually reviewed, locally cached real carousel images: three to five per place
- protected canonical Orthodox Sunday at Holy Myrrhbearers in Mount Crawford
- one planned social Roanoke night, with no club required
- one athletic/adrenaline-coded stop, Pyramid Mountain, scheduled before Skylands on the only real hiking day
- every expanded day remains at or below the 210-minute road-network baseline cap

## Why this route is different

Route 10 connects belief, machinery and strange architecture. A motorcycle collection lives inside a rescued textile mill; a monumental living Hindu temple turns volunteer craft into carved sacred space; Hagley's river-powered powder works expose the machinery behind a dynasty; Agecroft is a Tudor manor physically carried from Lancashire to Virginia; and Roanoke combines a sculptural museum, staged night-train photography, a neon mountain star and giant locomotives.

Roanoke is the endpoint because it produces a coherent reveal while preserving a genuinely different return through the Shenandoah Valley. Asheville was tested and rejected: the northbound chain could not return by October 14 without breaking the 3½-hour cap or turning multiple days into drive-only transfers.

The rhythm is intentionally balanced. Tight two-stop road days are followed by a spacious 135-minute Roanoke day and a calm 127-minute rail-and-pinball day. Orthodox worship is protected on Sunday. The return joins history, transport design, a short hike, a garden estate and two after-car-return Boston interiors without breaking the driving cap.

## Promoted hidden gems

The former fallback set is now part of the measured daily plan: Hill-Stead, Sayen Gardens, Winterthur, Government Island, Lewis Ginter, O. Winston Link Museum, Roanoke Pinball Museum, Carrier Arboretum, National Civil War Museum, Pyramid Mountain and Metropolitan Waterworks Museum.

Three overnight points moved to make the expanded route legal: East Milford/Orange, Baltimore's eastern waterfront and north Richmond. The stop order also matters. Winterthur comes before Hagley, the Civil War museum before AACA, and Pyramid Mountain before Skylands. Reversing those pairs would add unnecessary driving.

## Ratings, budget and images
