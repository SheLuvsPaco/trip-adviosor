## Why this route is different

The route is built around the tension between folklore and evidence. It begins gently with hand-carved carousel craft and a woodland megalith landscape, then moves through military archives, a roadside optical illusion and the exact Quecreek rescue field. Pittsburgh provides the visual crescendo: immigrant-built rooms hidden inside a Gothic skyscraper, a maximalist bicycle warehouse, outsider art and dinner inside a former church.

The emotional endpoint is not one distant city but a two-day Ohio Valley/Appalachian sequence. Point Pleasant joins the Mothman archive and statue to the documented Battle of Point Pleasant and the real World War II explosives landscape behind the TNT-area story. Sutton and Weston then move from playful cryptid museums into a daytime, history-only Trans-Allegheny tour and a quiet regional glass archive. That order lets the group enjoy folklore without confusing it with documented history or exploiting former patients as Halloween content.

The return deliberately softens: Orthodox Sunday in Morgantown, a small Bedford museum, a highway-perfect but genuinely surreal Cabela's destination, Tarrywile's mansion-and-meadow reset, then both cars returned before a transit-only Museum of Bad Art finale. It should feel like a complete narrative rather than eleven days of unrelated pins.
