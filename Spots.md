3. MYSTERY / HIDDEN HISTORY — 4 picks
⑦ Historic Occoneechee Speedway Trail
Address: 320 Elizabeth Brady Road, Hillsborough, NC 27278
Category: mystery-hidden-history (legally-visitable abandoned site)
Pitch: The only surviving dirt track from NASCAR's inaugural 1949 season, abandoned in 1968 and swallowed by forest for fifty years. You walk three miles of trail along the Eno River and come out onto a full oval where the grandstands are still standing under weeds with vintage Pepsi-Cola logos still legible on the boards, and rusted-out race cars sit in the infield with their roll cages, steering wheels and leather seats intact and open to look into. Richard Petty and Fireball Roberts raced here. It's on the National Register (one of only three racetracks that are), it became part of Eno River State Park in 2025, and it is completely free and completely legal — which is exactly the "abandoned place without trespassing" thing that's normally impossible to find. This is the antidote to a museum: it's the ruin itself, unrestored, with no docent.
Best-fit: Gora (a genuinely abandoned, decaying, forgotten piece of American history you can climb around in, plus quiet river woods to reset), Viki (the overgrown grandstands and rusted cars are the most photogenic thing on the southern half of the route and nothing like anything else on it).
Practical: Free. Gravel parking lot, clear signage. Daylight hours. 3 miles of trail across 44 acres — walk 1.5–2.5 hrs, or 45 min if you just do the oval. No reservation. Wear real shoes.
Slots into: Day 6 (Fri Oct 9), Durham — Hillsborough is ~15 min from Durham, and sits directly on the Roanoke→Durham approach via I-40/I-85. ~10 min detour, or a zero-detour morning-of stop.
Season: Outdoor, open year-round, no gate. October is peak condition (leaf color, no bugs, no heat). No closure risk.
Sources: https://www.dncr.nc.gov/explore/tripstravel-ideas/moonshine-and-motorsports-trail/moonshine-motorsports-trail-sites/occoneechee-speedway · https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/historic-occaneechee-speedway · https://www.wral.com/story/horse-races-and-nascar-exploring-the-remains-of-occoneechee-speedway/19166527/
⑧ Hollywood Cemetery — the W.W. Pool Mausoleum ("The Richmond Vampire")
Address: 412 South Cherry Street, Richmond, VA 23220
Category: mystery-hidden-history / folklore
Pitch: In 1925 the Church Hill railway tunnel collapsed on a work crew, and the story that spread through Richmond was that something came out of the rubble — bloody, skinless, teeth bared — and ran until it disappeared inside the W. W. Pool mausoleum in Hollywood Cemetery. The tomb is still there, dated 1913, covered in mixed Masonic and Egyptian iconography, with a doubled "WW" carved above the door that reads unmistakably as fangs. The legend has a documented, traceable origin (a real 1925 disaster and a real disfigured survivor) that got folded into vampire folklore within days — so you can tell it honestly as folklore and still have the actual history underneath it, which is exactly the register Gora asked for. The cemetery itself is a 135-acre terraced Victorian landscape falling down bluffs straight into the James River rapids, with two U.S. presidents and a cast-iron pyramid in it.
Best-fit: Gora (documented-yet-legendary; the honest-folklore case, with a real industrial disaster at the bottom of it), Viki (the bluff-over-the-rapids overlook and the iron dog grave are among the most photographed spots in Richmond, and it is free).
Practical: Free (donations encouraged). Open daily 8am–6pm during Daylight Saving Time — DST is still in effect on Oct 10–11, 2026, so you get the 6pm close. Self-guided, 1–1.5 hrs; a 90-min guided historical walking tour runs Saturday mornings via The Valentine if you want a docent. No reservation for self-guided. (804) 648-8501.
Slots into: Day 7 (Sat Oct 10) evening or Day 8 (Sun Oct 11) morning, Richmond — in the overnight city, ~10 min from downtown. Zero detour. Day 8 morning after the Orthodox service is the natural slot and does not touch the fixed service.
Season: Open daily year-round. No closure risk. Only note: closing drops to 5pm once DST ends in November — not a factor for these dates.
Sources: https://www.hollywoodcemetery.org/visit · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond_Vampire · https://wjla.com/amazing-america/discover-the-eerie-legend-of-the-richmond-vampire-at-hollywood-cemetery
⑨ Eastern State Penitentiary
Address: 2027 Fairmount Avenue, Philadelphia, PA 19130
Category: mystery-hidden-history
Pitch: A ten-acre ruined prison in the middle of Philadelphia — vaulted cellblocks radiating from a central hub, collapsed roofs, trees growing through the floors, and Al Capone's actual cell, preserved. What lifts it above "old prison tour" is what it's actually about: Eastern State invented modern solitary confinement as a humanitarian reform, and the site now runs a permanent exhibit that connects that experiment directly to present-day U.S. mass incarceration — an institution publicly and unflinchingly indicting its own founding idea. That's the genuinely uncomfortable, narrative-contradicting history Gora is after, and it's in a building that looks like a Piranesi engraving. Note that in October the site also runs Halloween Nights (Oct 2–Nov 7, 2026) after dark, so the daytime visit shares the site with elaborate haunt sets going up — either a bonus or a distraction depending on taste.
Best-fit: Gora (institutional history that contradicts the official story, told by the institution itself), Viki (decaying vaulted cellblocks with light coming through collapsed ceilings — the most striking photography on the northbound leg).
Practical: Open daily 10am–5pm in October, including Mondays. Admission not published on the hours page — budget ~$20–25/person; audio tour narrated by Steve Buscemi is included with admission. 1.5–2.5 hrs. Timed tickets recommended online. Halloween Nights is a separate, much more expensive ticket — you want the daytime historic admission, not Halloween Nights.
Slots into: Day 9 (Mon Oct 12) — on the Hagley (Wilmington DE) → Bensalem PA leg, which runs straight through Philadelphia anyway. ~15–20 min detour off I-95 into Fairmount.
Season: Open daily in October. Confirmed Monday-open. The one caveat: confirm no daytime closure on that specific date once the Halloween Nights calendar is published (dates go up in late summer).
Sources: https://easternstate.org/historic-site/hours-of-operation · https://easternstate.org/halloween/schedule-pricing · https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g60795-d23585580-Reviews-Halloween_Nights_at_Eastern_State_Penitentiary-Philadelphia_Pennsylvania.html
⑩ Harpers Ferry National Historical Park — John Brown's Fort & the Storer College / Niagara Movement site
Address: 171 Shoreline Drive, Harpers Ferry, WV 25425 (visitor center); Lower Town / John Brown's Fort at Shenandoah St & Potomac St
Category: mystery-hidden-history (and doubles as photogenic-unusual)
Pitch: This is the most legitimately contested history site on the route. John Brown's 1859 raid — an armed insurrection intended to arm enslaved people — was called terrorism, martyrdom, and treason within the same decade, and how it gets taught has swung violently ever since; the little brick fire-engine house he barricaded himself into is still standing and you can walk up to it. On the hill above, Storer College — one of the first colleges in the U.S. open to Black students — hosted the 1906 Niagara Movement conference, W.E.B. Du Bois's direct rebuke of the accommodationist line that dominated the official story for the next fifty years; the campus is part of the park. Layer on a town that was destroyed and rebuilt eight times in the Civil War and then abandoned to floods, sitting in the gap where two rivers collide — Jefferson Rock and the confluence overlook are as good as an East Coast photograph gets in mid-October.
Best-fit: Gora (suppressed, reversed, and re-reversed American history at the actual site, plus quiet river gorge), Viki (the confluence overlook, Jefferson Rock, and the stone-and-ruin Lower Town streets are pure photograph), Stivka (easy walking, nature, low-key).
Practical: Park open daily; Lower Town buildings generally 9am–5pm. ~$20 per vehicle, valid 3 days (or free with an America the Beautiful pass — worth buying if the route hits other federal sites). No reservation. 2–3 hrs for Lower Town + the walk up to Jefferson Rock (well under the 5–6 mile hike cap; the Jefferson Rock walk is under a mile round trip).
Slots into: Day 4 (Wed Oct 7) — Harpers Ferry is directly on the Lancaster→Winchester line via US-340. Effectively zero detour, and it shares a parking area with River Riders (candidate ④), so the two combine into one strong Day 4 that replaces the weakest existing stop on the route (see §5).
Season: Open year-round. Mid-October is peak foliage in the gorge — the best possible timing. No closure risk.
Sources: https://www.nps.gov/hafe/index.htm · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harpers_Ferry_National_Historical_Park · https://northernvirginiamag.com/things-to-do/travel/2026/06/10/5-summer-outdoor-adventures-in-harpers-ferry/

4. PHOTOGENIC-UNUSUAL & METAL/ROCK — 3 picks
⑪ SteelStacks / Hoover-Mason Trestle
Address: 711 East 1st Street, Bethlehem, PA 18015 (Bethlehem Visitor Center entrance)
Category: photogenic-unusual (+ metal-rock adjacent)
Pitch: Five Bethlehem Steel blast furnaces, dead since 1995, left standing exactly where they were — and a 2,000-foot elevated walkway 46 feet up, the old narrow-gauge ore trestle, that runs you right along their flanks at furnace height. At night the whole stack complex is floodlit and it is one of the great industrial photographs in the eastern United States, and it costs nothing. This is where American steel for the Golden Gate Bridge and the Navy's WWII fleet came from, and the campus deliberately refused to demolish or prettify it. The adjacent Musikfest Café at ArtsQuest programs live music year-round in the shadow of the furnaces, so it can double as the evening.
Best-fit: Viki (this is the single most photogenic "infrastructure" subject on the route and precisely her stated taste — striking architecture, one photo captures the memory), Stivka (live music venue inside a dead steel mill), Gora (real industrial history, and it's a free walk).
Practical: Free. Trestle open daily, free parking at the Visitor Center lot or Wind Creek. 45–75 min. No reservation. Best at dusk/after dark for the lit furnaces — which fits perfectly with arriving in Bethlehem in the evening.
Slots into: Day 2 (Mon Oct 5), Bethlehem — in the overnight city. Zero detour. Pairs naturally with escape.exe or Lehigh Valley Grand Prix the same evening.
Season: Open daily, year-round, outdoors. Check steelstacks.org for event-day closures on the campus. Minimal risk.
Sources: https://www.steelstacks.org/hoover-mason-trestle/ · https://uncoveringpa.com/hoover-mason-trestle-bethlehem · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoover-Mason_Trestle
⑫ GWARbar
Address: 217 W Clay St, Richmond, VA 23220 (Jackson Ward)
Category: metal-rock
Pitch: An actual bar owned and operated by GWAR — the Richmond-born scumdog metal band — in the city where they formed. The interior is fake-blood-splattered, hung with band artifacts and props, the menu is a joke-serious mix of BBQ and genuinely good vegan pub food ("Meat Yo Maker"), and it functions as the living room of Richmond's heavy scene rather than a tourist trap. This is a rock-history site you can drink in: GWAR is one of the most singular things American metal has ever produced, and this is their home bar in their home city. There is nothing else remotely like it anywhere on this route, and it is the single most on-target thing on this route for Stivka.
Best-fit: Stivka (the metal pick of the trip — a real band-owned bar in a real scene city, not a themed chain), Gora (drink, party, meet people — his stated other side), Viki (unusual venue, extremely photographable, and it's a fun night rather than a museum).

⑬ Roanoke Pinball Museum
Address: 1 Market Square SE, 2nd Floor, Roanoke, VA 24011 (Center in the Square)
Category: photogenic-unusual

It is loud, competitive, hands-on, dirt cheap, and in downtown Roanoke on the overnight square. Ten years running.
Best-fit: Viki (unusual, tactile, and the wall of lit backglasses is a genuinely great photograph), Gora & Stivka (competitive, high-score trash talk, drinks nearby), Paco (a stop where all four are actively doing the same thing).

Best fit: Gora (institutional history the state itself was forced to condemn — verified, not conspiratorial); Viki (photographically it's the most striking interior on the route).
Practical: From $14–15/adult; 90 minutes; all ages; day tours run April 2 – Nov 30, in season at 10:00, 12:00, 2:00 and 4:00 Tue–Fri, top-of-hour on weekends. Book online. Photography tour ($100, 4 hrs, 18+) exists if Viki wants unrestricted shooting time.
Slots into: Day 5 (overnight Wheeling), bundled with E1 above. ~20 min south of Wheeling.

M2. Grave Creek Mound & Delf Norona Museum — and the Grave Creek Stone
Address: 801 Jefferson Ave, Moundsville, WV 26041 (directly across the street from the penitentiary)
Category: mystery-hidden-history
Pitch: A 62-ft-tall conical Adena burial mound, built roughly 250–150 BC, one of the largest of its kind in the Americas — and it sits in the middle of a small Ohio Valley town, facing a Gothic prison, which is one of the more surreal urban juxtapositions in the eastern US. The hook for Gora is the Grave Creek Stone: a small inscribed sandstone tablet recovered from the upper burial vault in the 1838 excavation, carrying characters nobody has convincingly read. Its authenticity has been argued over for nearly two centuries, the original is now missing, and only four casts at the Smithsonian survive. This is a real, live, unresolved archaeological controversy — presentable honestly, with the mainstream "it's a 19th-century forgery" reading and the dissent, which is precisely his register.
Best fit: Gora (contested archaeology, a physically missing artifact, honest folklore-vs-evidence framing); Stivka/Viki (the mound itself is a striking, easy, free walk-up).
Practical: Museum Tue–Sat 9:00am–4:30pm, closed Sun/Mon; free or nominal admission (WV state historic site); 45–60 minutes including walking the spiral path to the top of the mound.
Slots into: Day 5 — same block as M1/E1, so 0 additional detour beyond the Moundsville trip. Thursday 10/8 is within Tue–Sat.
Season: Year-round, no closure risk. Mound grounds walkable regardless.
Sources: WV Culture — Grave Creek Mound · NPS — Grave Creek Mound · Tripadvisor · e-WV encyclopedia
M3. The Pump House / Battle of Homestead site
Address: 880 E Waterfront Dr, Munhall, PA 15120
Category: mystery-hidden-history
Pitch: The last standing building of Carnegie's Homestead Steel Works, and the exact spot where on July 6, 1892 locked-out steelworkers fought a pitched gun battle against 300 armed Pinkerton agents floating up the Monongahela on barges — ten dead, the state militia occupying the town, and the union broken in American steel for the next forty years. It is a shooting-war between a private corporate army and its own workforce, on American soil, and it is not what most people were taught. The Pump House is free, open dawn to dusk, and sits beside the riverbank the barges landed on. Crucially it is open on a Thursday, which the Carrie Blast Furnaces are not.
Best fit: Gora (suppressed/underemphasized labor history, documented to the hour); Paco-balance (free, 30 minutes, doesn't cost the day).
Practical: Free (small donation for parking); dusk-till-dawn access, closed in winter months; ~30–45 min self-guided; no reservation. Group tours bookable via Rivers of Steel, 412-464-4020.
Slots into: Day 5, after Bayernhof. Bayernhof (O'Hara Twp) → Munhall ~20–25 min, and Munhall is broadly on the southern exit toward I-79/I-70 for Wheeling — ~20–25 min net detour.
Season: Open in October; outdoor/riverside site, weather-dependent only.
Sources: Rivers of Steel — Pump House · Battle of Homestead Foundation · Steel Valley Trail Council · Secret Pittsburgh (Univ. of Pittsburgh)
Strongly related, flagged: Carrie Blast Furnaces National Historic Landmark (801 Carrie Furnace Blvd, Rankin PA). M4. Warren Anatomical Museum — the skull of Phineas Gage
Address: Countway Library of Medicine, 5th floor, 10 Shattuck St, Boston, MA 02115
Category: mystery-hidden-history
Pitch: On the fifth floor of Harvard's medical library, free, in a quiet gallery almost nobody knows exists, sits the actual skull of Phineas Gage — the railroad foreman who in 1848 had a 13-pound, 3½-foot tamping iron blown clean through his left frontal lobe and walked away, whose personality reportedly changed so completely that his friends said he was "no longer Gage," and whose case founded the entire modern understanding that personality lives in the brain. The tamping iron itself is displayed alongside it. Surrounding it: a 19th-century collection of anatomical and surgical specimens, and the history of a medical school that acquired bodies in ways it does not entirely enjoy discussing. It is a 10-minute walk-in that produces a longer conversation than most $40 attractions.
Best fit: Gora (medicine's uncomfortable history + a genuinely mind-altering documented case); Viki (macabre-unusual, and it is free); the group (it's short — no one has to commit a half-day).
Practical: Free; Mon–Fri 9am–5pm, closed weekends, holidays, and academic recesses; show photo ID at the Countway security desk and sign the register; groups over 7 must make an appointment — with four travelers you're under that, but call 617-432-6196 to confirm the gallery is open that week. 30–45 minutes.
Slots into: Day 11, Boston — pairs directly with the Harvard Museum of Natural History Glass Flowers. Cambridge → Longwood is ~15–20 min. Day 11 is Wednesday Oct 14, inside the Mon–Fri window.
Season: No seasonal risk. Risk: closes during Harvard academic recesses — mid-October is not a standard recess, but verify.

M5. Union Cemetery — the White Lady of Easton
Address: Intersection of Rt 59 & Rt 111 (Stepney Rd), Easton, CT 06612
Category: mystery-hidden-history / folklore
Pitch: A 400-year-old New England burying ground with 17th-century slate headstones, and the site of America's most-reported roadside apparition: the White Lady, a figure in a white nightgown and bonnet repeatedly described by drivers on Route 59 — including, notably, by on-duty Easton firefighters and police, one of whom reported striking her with a vehicle and finding a dent but no body. Ed and Lorraine Warren investigated it for decades and claimed to have filmed her. The value here is that it can be presented completely honestly as folklore-with-a-documented-report-trail: nobody has to pretend it's proven, and the headstone-and-stone-wall setting is genuinely lovely in October light. Fifteen minutes and free.
Best fit: Gora explicitly — this is his "folklore/cryptid/legend, presented honestly as legend" bucket; Viki (autumn New England graveyard is a photograph).
Practical: Free; daytime only — entering after dark is trespassing and Easton police enforce it, so this must be scheduled in daylight; 20–30 minutes; no reservation.
Slots into: Day 10, overnight Danbury. Easton is ~25 min south of Danbury — ~50 min round-trip detour, which is above the 15–20 min guideline. Honest call: only include if Day 10 stays light (Tarrywile + Hearthstone + Escapology is already a reasonable day). Treat as a hidden-gem swap candidate rather than a core stop.

M6. Gobbler's Knob & Phil's Burrow, Punxsutawney

Category: mystery-hidden-history / photogenic-unusual (Americana folklore)

4. PHOTOGENIC / UNUSUAL (Viki) and METAL / ROCK (Stivka)
P1. Opus 40
Address: 356 George Sickle Rd, Saugerties, NY 12477
Category: photogenic-unusual
Pitch: A sculptor named Harvey Fite spent 37 years, alone, hand-fitting bluestone without mortar into a 6.5-acre earthwork built into an abandoned quarry — ramps, terraces, pools and a nine-ton monolith he raised by himself with a hand-built boom and winch, using drystone technique he learned restoring Mayan ruins at Copán. He died in 1976 falling from it. It is a single obsessive human's life laid out in stone, with Overlook Mountain framed behind it, and it is one of the most photographable places in the entire Hudson Valley. It also rhymes hard with the route's actual spine: a silo-sized kaleidoscope, scripture carved into boulders, a mansion of self-playing machines, and now a man who quarried his own cathedral. Opus 40 belongs on this route more than the Taber Museum does.
Best fit: Viki, unambiguously — this is her top-two bucket (unusual and photogenic in one stop); Gora (a quiet, physical, obsessive human achievement, and the grounds are a genuine nature reset).
Practical: $10 adult; open Thursday–Monday 10am–5pm; ~1–1.5 hours; no reservation needed for general admission.
Slots into: Day 2 (Ghent → Mount Tremper → Hancock). Saugerties is ~25 min from Mount Tremper and roughly on the line down from Ghent — ~20 min net detour. Day 2 is Monday Oct 5, which is inside the Thu–Mon window (Tue/Wed would have killed it).
Season: Open in October; outdoor site, so rain is the only risk. Note: an Opus 40 gala is scheduled Sat Oct 10 — doesn't affect Oct 5.

P2. Olana State Historic Site
Address: 5720 State Route 9G, Hudson, NY 12534
Category: photogenic-unusual
Pitch: Hudson River School painter Frederic Church built himself a Persian-Moorish fantasy villa on a hilltop above the Hudson — stencilled polychrome, Islamic arches, ochre and terracotta — and then landscaped 250 acres of carriage roads so that every bend frames a composed view, because he was designing the property itself as a three-dimensional painting. The result is a building that looks like nothing else in North America, sitting above a valley view that was deliberately engineered to be the best one available. The 250-acre landscape is free and open daily 8am to sunset, so you can get the entire photographic payoff without paying for or scheduling a house tour.
Best fit: Viki (striking architecture + engineered sunset viewpoint — this is her single best photograph on Days 1–2); Stivka (easy, pretty, low-commitment); Gora (grounds walk).

5. The two most redundant existing stops
Cut candidate #1 — Thomas T. Taber Museum, Williamsport (Day 3)
This is the weakest entry on the route and the clearest cut. It is a county historical society: regional artifacts, period rooms, and a model-train collection. Three problems compound:

It is generic by construction. Every county on this route has one; nothing about Taber is specific to this trip's premise. Set it next to a silo kaleidoscope, scripture carved into boulders, and a mansion of self-playing music machines and it reads as filler.
Its one hook is duplicated ~90 minutes off the Day 5/6 path — the Kruger Street Toy & Train Museum in Wheeling occupies the same "toy trains in a historic building" niche, in a city the route already sleeps in.
Its other content overlaps Day 8. "Small Pennsylvania town, Victorian-era wealth, period interiors" is exactly what the Bellefonte Victorian stroll already delivers, and Bellefonte does it outdoors and for free. Meanwhile Day 3 is a Tuesday with 150–210 min of drive budget and one stop, and the corridor's best Tuesday-available alternatives (Billtown Escape Rooms, Mon–Sat) are sitting unused. Recommendation: cut Taber outright, or demote it to a hidden-gem swap slot. It also happens to be a museum on a route that is trying to lower museum density, and it scores as a museum with no adrenaline, no mystery, and no photograph.
Cut candidate #2 — Butler Institute of American Art, Youngstown (Day 7)
Harder call — the Butler is a genuinely good regional museum with a real collection and free admission, and it is the only cultural anchor in Youngstown. But on this route it is the fourth "go look at art" stop: Art Omi (Day 1, sculpture park), Alan Cottrill's bronze studio (Day 6), Otherworld (Day 6, immersive art), Columcille (Day 9, sculptural stone environment) — and Opus 40 if it's added on Day 2. Under the Magic scoring model's equal-per-entity means, stacking a fifth art entity dilutes rather than adds. It is also the least distinctive of them: Cottrill lets you watch bronze being poured, Otherworld is a walk-through hallucination, Columcille is a stone sanctuary you're inside of. The Butler is paintings on walls.
Recommendation: demote rather than cut. Keep it as the daytime anchor (it's free and it's in the right city), but stop treating it as Day 7's headline — Day 7 should headline Youngstown Axe + Westside Bowl, with the Butler as the afternoon warm-up. That single reframing converts the route's most generic museum day into its best night, at zero additional driving.

A third, softer note: if Opus 40 is added on Day 2, someone should decide whether Art Omi on Day 1 still earns its slot, or whether Day 1 becomes the Olana day instead. I would keep both — they're meaningfully different — but flag it so the decision is made deliberately rather than discovered in the scoring.
