## Design Context

### Users
Four friends use this as a private, shared-device decision room on a large screen, then on mobile for individual ratings. They need to feel each road-trip option, understand its practical compromises, compare routes, and choose together without hiding driving, weather, cost, or operational risk.

### Brand Personality
Crafted, warm, mysterious, exact. The interface should combine the quiet authority of a printed road atlas, the drama of a premium travel documentary, the precision of a planning table, and the intimacy of friends spreading route notes across one table.

### Aesthetic Direction
Map-first editorial travel atlas. Use deep graphite/night ocean, warm paper surfaces, brass accents, restrained route colors, characterful serif display type, refined sans-serif UI type, and sparse monospaced operational metadata. Avoid corporate SaaS dashboards, booking marketplaces, generic card grids, sci-fi HUDs, neon AI styling, and Google Maps imitation.

### Design Principles
1. Let the map and story panel stay in dialogue.
2. Lead with emotion, then progressively reveal exact operational detail.
3. Give each color one job: route identity, Magic/premium emphasis, or risk status.
4. Treat itinerary days as chapters rather than spreadsheet rows.
5. Keep researched caveats, empty ratings, and replacements honest and visible.
